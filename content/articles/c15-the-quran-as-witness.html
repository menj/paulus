The Qurʾān never names Paul. It names, with precision, the doctrines he introduced, the method by which scripture was altered, and the words of ʿĪsā ibn Maryam (ʿalayhi al-salām, peace be upon him) that his followers set aside.

The Muslim scholars who identified Paul as the corrupter, from Sayf ibn ʿUmar to Isma'il R. al Faruqi, are treated in [paulus_link slug="paul-in-the-islamic-tradition"]The Islamic tradition[/paulus_link].1 This article sets out the revelation they were reading.

Translations below follow Saheeh International; the Arabic is given so that the reader may check them.2

The crucifixion

[paulus_link slug="seven-doctrines"]Count·III[/paulus_link] sets out Paul's doctrine that the death of ʿĪsā ibn Maryam on the cross redeems the sin of humanity.3 The Qurʾān denies the premise.

وَقَوْلِهِمْ إِنَّا قَتَلْنَا ٱلْمَسِيحَ عِيسَى ٱبْنَ مَرْيَمَ رَسُولَ ٱللَّهِ وَمَا قَتَلُوهُ وَمَا صَلَبُوهُ وَلَـٰكِن شُبِّهَ لَهُمْ

"And [for] their saying, 'Indeed, we have killed the Messiah, Jesus, the son of Mary, the messenger of Allah.' And they did not kill him, nor did they crucify him; but [another] was made to resemble him to them."4

Without the crucifixion there is no redeeming sacrifice, no Passover lamb, no hilastērion; the whole edifice of [paulus_link slug="typology"]Count·V's typology[/paulus_link] rests on an event the Qurʾān says did not occur.5

The modern reformer Rashīd Riḍā tested the Christian account by the standard Muslim scholarship applies to every report. The story of the crucifixion, he concluded, "has no connected chain" (sanad muttaṣil) back to the people it is reported from, and those who reported it "are not known with certainty".6

The divinity

Paul calls ʿĪsā ibn Maryam "the image of God," and the letters written in his name make him the image of the invisible God in whom the fullness of the Godhead dwells bodily.7 The Qurʾān answers the claim and its later development in one breath.

لَقَدْ كَفَرَ ٱلَّذِينَ قَالُوٓا۟ إِنَّ ٱللَّهَ هُوَ ٱلْمَسِيحُ ٱبْنُ مَرْيَمَ ۖ وَقَالَ ٱلْمَسِيحُ يَـٰبَنِىٓ إِسْرَٰٓءِيلَ ٱعْبُدُوا۟ ٱللَّهَ رَبِّى وَرَبَّكُمْ

"They have certainly disbelieved who say, 'Allah is the Messiah, the son of Mary' while the Messiah has said, 'O Children of Israel, worship Allah, my Lord and your Lord.'"8

لَّقَدْ كَفَرَ ٱلَّذِينَ قَالُوٓا۟ إِنَّ ٱللَّهَ ثَالِثُ ثَلَـٰثَةٍ ۘ وَمَا مِنْ إِلَـٰهٍ إِلَّآ إِلَـٰهٌ وَٰحِدٌ

"They have certainly disbelieved who say, 'Allah is the third of three.' And there is no god except one God."9

The verse sets the words of ʿĪsā ibn Maryam against the words said about him, which is the method of this whole site. His own confession, in the Qurʾān, is the confession of a servant: "Indeed, I am the servant of Allah.

He has given me the Scripture and made me a prophet."10 On the Day of Judgment he will be asked whether he told the people to take him and his mother as deities, and will answer that he said nothing except what he was commanded: "worship Allah, my Lord and your Lord."11

Lords besides God

The Qurʾān describes the mechanism by which a religion of servants became a religion of lords.

ٱتَّخَذُوٓا۟ أَحْبَارَهُمْ وَرُهْبَـٰنَهُمْ أَرْبَابًا مِّن دُونِ ٱللَّهِ وَٱلْمَسِيحَ ٱبْنَ مَرْيَمَ وَمَآ أُمِرُوٓا۟ إِلَّا لِيَعْبُدُوٓا۟ إِلَـٰهًا وَٰحِدًا

"They have taken their scholars and monks as lords besides Allah, and [also] the Messiah, the son of Mary. And they were not commanded except to worship one God."12

The verse names two steps: the exaltation of religious authorities into lawgivers, and the exaltation of al-Masīḥ into an object of worship. [paulus_link slug="paul-the-father-of-a-new-religion"]Count·VI[/paulus_link] traces both, from the letters to the councils.13 The Qurʾān adds the institution that Paul's theology of the body produced, monasticism, and says of it that they invented it; God did not prescribe it.14

Altering the scripture

[paulus_link slug="twisting-the-scriptures"]Count·V[/paulus_link] documents the verses of the Tanakh that Paul's letters changed in the quoting.15 The Qurʾān describes the practice as a practice.

وَإِنَّ مِنْهُمْ لَفَرِيقًا يَلْوُۥنَ أَلْسِنَتَهُم بِٱلْكِتَـٰبِ لِتَحْسَبُوهُ مِنَ ٱلْكِتَـٰبِ وَمَا هُوَ مِنَ ٱلْكِتَـٰبِ

"And indeed, there is among them a party who alter the Scripture with their tongues so you may think it is from the Scripture, but it is not from the Scripture."16

Of those who called themselves Christians it says that a covenant was taken from them, and that they forgot a portion of what they had been reminded of, so that enmity and hatred were cast among them until the Day of Resurrection.17 The Verdict quotes the sharpest form of the charge, on those who write the scripture with their own hands and say that it is from God.18
