Why “Apostle of Doom”

“So woe to those who write the scripture with their own hands, then say, ‘This is from God.’”1 The name of this site answers the man who did exactly that. This note sets out why we chose it, in shāʾ Allāh (God willing).

The name has stood since 2011, and the book behind this site carries it as its English title.2 Its first word is Paul’s claim. Its second is our verdict, in four counts.

“Apostle” is the title Paul gave himself. He wrote that he was an apostle “not of men, neither by man”, and that he received his gospel from no one: “neither was I taught it, but by the revelation of Jesus Christ”.3

We take him at his word. He was an “apostle” appointed by no one who had known ʿĪsā ibn Maryam, ʿalayhi al-salām (peace be upon him), and answerable to none of them.

“Doom” names, first, what his teaching did to that prophet’s message. ʿĪsā ʿalayhi al-salām came to the Children of Israel with the worship of the One God and obedience to His law; the gospel Paul preached in its place set the law aside and made its messenger an object of worship. [paulus_link slug="the-charges"]The charges[/paulus_link] trace that ruin, count by count.

It names, too, the end awaiting those who follow him. The Prophet ﷺ warned, in a ḥadīth (a report of his words) recorded by al-Tirmidhī, that on the Day of Resurrection the arrogant will be driven into سِجْنٍ فِي جَهَنَّمَ يُسَمَّى بُولَسَ (sijnin fī jahannama yusammā Būlus), “a prison in Hell called Būlus”.4

The report speaks of arrogance and names no man. Būlus is nonetheless the Arabic form of Paul’s name, and the arrogance it punishes is the sin of a man who appointed himself. The book weighs the echo in its account of [paulus_link slug="the-early-islamic-record"]the early Islamic record[/paulus_link].

The third count we argue ex concessis, from Paul’s own admissions: al-iḥtijāj bi-kalām al-khaṣm ʿalayh (الاحتجاج بكلام الخصم عليه), arguing against an opponent with his own words, the method that runs through the book.

The New Testament’s word for final ruin is ἀπώλεια, destruction or perdition: the broad way “that leadeth to destruction”, which many enter.5 Paul turned it on those who opposed him, men “whose end is destruction”.6 The name turns it back on him.

Last, Paul was a herald of doom in the plainest sense: he foretold an end within his own lifetime. He told the Thessalonians that “we which are alive and remain unto the coming of the Lord” would be caught up to meet him in the air, and the Corinthians that “the time is short”, since “the fashion of this world passeth away”.7 He died, as did every one of his hearers, and the world went on. [paulus_link slug="the-failed-prophet"]The failed prophet[/paulus_link] sets out the record.

The verdict

The name holds the case in nuce: an “apostle” of his own making, whose message ended in ruin. Of such writing the Qurʾān has already spoken: “woe to them for what their hands have written.”8
