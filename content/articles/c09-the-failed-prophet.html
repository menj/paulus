Rejection of Paul came first from the disciples and the early movements such as the Ebionites, and in time from Western thinkers who owed nothing to Islam, whose testimony [paulus_link slug="the-hostile-witnesses"]The hostile witnesses[/paulus_link] gathers. This part turns from what others have said of Paul to what his own words foretold.

A prophecy that failed

Ironically, the founder of the new teaching that dominates church theology ended his life in a scene unlike the noble picture of a heavenly messenger. The Bible itself refutes Paul's claim to be one of the disciples. Consider the following verse:

16ὅτι αὐτὸς ὁ κύριος ἐν κελεύσματι, ἐν φωνῇ ἀρχαγγέλου καὶ ἐν σάλπιγγι θεοῦ, καταβήσεται ἀπʼ οὐρανοῦ, καὶ οἱ νεκροὶ ἐν Χριστῷ ἀναστήσονται πρῶτον, 17ἔπειτα ἡμεῖς οἱ ζῶντες οἱ περιλειπόμενοι ἅμα σὺν αὐτοῖς ἁρπαγησόμεθα ἐν νεφέλαις εἰς ἀπάντησιν τοῦ κυρίου εἰς ἀέρα· καὶ οὕτως πάντοτε σὺν κυρίῳ ἐσόμεθα.

16For the Lord himself shall descend from heaven with a shout, with the voice of the archangel, and with the trump of God: and the dead in Christ shall rise first: 17Then we which are alive and remain shall be caught up together with them in the clouds, to meet the Lord in the air: and so shall we ever be with the Lord.1

Paul's prediction plainly states that he and the Christians still alive at the time would be taken up by the Lord (Jesus Christ) and go together to meet him.2 That is more than spiritual hope.

It is a form of prophecy written in the name of "the word of the Lord." New Testament scholars such as Bart D. Ehrman3 and Albert Schweitzer4 acknowledge that Paul expected the parousia (παρουσία), a Greek term meaning "coming" or "presence" that refers to the reappearance of Christ in the apocalyptic tradition, within his own generation.

That is not what happened. What happened instead was the sword that "took him up" and parted his head from his body in the reign of Nero, around 64 to 68 CE.5

By the standard the Torah itself sets, a prophet who speaks in the name of the LORD, and the thing does not come to pass, has spoken presumptuously, and is not to be feared:

22When a prophet speaketh in the name of the LORD, if the thing follow not, nor come to pass, that is the thing which the LORD hath not spoken, but the prophet hath spoken it presumptuously: thou shalt not be afraid of him.6

Textually and ethically, then, Paul meets the criterion of a false prophet, for delivering in the name of God a prediction that did not come true.

The same chapter of the Torah names the end of such a man. Of the prophet who presumes to speak in God's name a word that God has not commanded, it says: "even that prophet shall die."7

The Prophet Muhammad ﷺ died an old man, at the age of sixty-three.8 Paul died under the executioner's sword, beheaded in Rome.9

Paul's end therefore fails the Torah's test twice over. The prophecy he gave in the name of the Lord never came to pass, and he died as the Torah says the presumptuous prophet dies.
