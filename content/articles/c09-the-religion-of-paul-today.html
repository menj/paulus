Now that it is plain that the roots of modern Christianity lie in Paul and not in ʿĪsā ibn Maryam (ʿalayhi al-salām, peace be upon him), it becomes an intellectual duty for those who seek the truth to distinguish between the message of heaven and the invention of man. The view of a churchman such as John Shelby Spong, an Anglican bishop who long wrestled with the contradictions of the Christian tradition, cannot be set aside. He insisted that Paul's teachings carry no claim to absolute revelation:

Paul's words are not the Words of God. They are the words of Paul, a vast difference.1

Spong challenged Paul's absolute authority in theology, and he also showed that many of Paul's injunctions, the demeaning of women, the sanctioning of slavery, the exaltation of political power, reflect the views of a man trapped in a cultural frame long since abandoned. In his view, unless Paul's writings are freed from "the crust of first-century culture," they become a moral burden and no spiritual grace.

Teachings such as "let your women keep silence in the churches," "servants, obey your masters" and "every ruler is the minister of God" reflect an age full of prejudice, and if defended literally they turn religion into a tool of oppression hiding behind revelation. So, Spong adds, whoever exalts these words too far as scripture will end by destroying their own spiritual meaning, since "those who claim too much will finally achieve nothing."

فَوَيْلٌ لِّلَّذِينَ يَكْتُبُونَ ٱلْكِتَٰبَ بِأَيْدِيهِمْ ثُمَّ يَقُولُونَ هَٰذَا مِنْ عِندِ ٱللَّهِ

"So woe to those who write the scripture with their own hands, then say, 'This is from God.'"2

The difference between the tawḥīd brought by ʿĪsā al-Masīḥ and the Religion of Paul is a total separation between two roads: one sourced in heaven, the other born of a crisis of the soul.

Blood at the center

22And almost all things are by the law purged with blood; and without shedding of blood is no remission.5
