## Barnabas and Paul

Throughout the spreading of the teaching of ʿĪsā ibn Maryam, Barnabas traveled widely with Paul, who now appeared in a new guise as the most faithful adherent of that teaching. Paul began to introduce himself as "servant of Christ Jesus" or "apostle of Christ Jesus," as the openings of his letters show.[[fn:cf. Romans 1:1; 1 Corinthians 1:1; Galatians 1:1, etc.]]

The relationship between Barnabas and Paul is described in Acts:

{{kjv:Acts 13:1-2}}

In the list of the disciples, Luke names Barnabas first and places Saul (Paul) last.

Chosen to work together, the disciples set out to spread the teaching. Barnabas and Paul left with Mark to carry the teaching of ʿĪsā ibn Maryam into Greek territory, while James the son of Zebedee remained in Palestine as head of the followers of ʿĪsā ibn Maryam, together with Peter.

Acts also records that although they were sometimes stoned in various places,[[fn:Acts 14:19.]] the work of the two preachers bore great fruit, and their reputation as messengers of God spread widely.

Not long after they had spread the mission of ʿĪsā ibn Maryam together, Barnabas began to distance himself from Paul, having recognized the underhand tactic by which Paul was trying to compromise the religion of ʿĪsā ibn Maryam with the beliefs of the Graeco-Roman world. How Paul went on to fit the teaching of ʿĪsā ibn Maryam to the beliefs of the Graeco-Roman world is the third count against him, set out in [paulus_link slug="the-foundations-of-pauline-doctrine" /].
