A case needs a measure. This site judges Paul of Tarsus by principles stated before the charges are heard, open to inspection by Muslim and Christian alike, and applied to the evidence without favour. Each is drawn from the Qurʾān and the Sunnah, and each can be tested by reason. This page sets them out, <em>in shāʾ Allāh</em> (God willing).

## Understanding before judgment

Judgment comes second. We first let Paul speak at length, in his own letters and in the book written about him, and we take his doctrines as his followers hold them. Only then do we weigh them, and we weigh them by principles that belong to neither party, since a standard borrowed from either side would decide the case before it was heard.

## The unity of truth

The first principle is <em>tawḥīd</em> (التوحيد, the affirmation of God's oneness), and with it the unity of truth. If God is One, truth is one, and what contradicts itself cannot come from Him. The Qurʾān offers itself to that very test: "If it had been from [any] other than Allah, they would have found within it much contradiction."[[fn:Sūrah al-Nisāʾ 4:82.]] Three rules of judgment follow. A claim must correspond with reality. It must not contradict itself. It must stay open to evidence, for the Qurʾān forbids pursuing "that of which you have no knowledge", and asks every claimant: "Produce your proof, if you should be truthful."[[fn:Sūrah al-Isrāʾ 17:36; Sūrah al-Baqarah 2:111.]]

Transcendence stands at the heart of the principle. Every human is created upon the <em>fiṭrah</em> (فطرة, the natural disposition), "the fitrah of Allah upon which He has created [all] people", and by it knows that God is, that He is One, and that no creature shares His nature.[[fn:Sūrah al-Rūm 30:30.]] Paul's Christ, a man "in the form of God" before whose name every knee must bow, fails that test before any other.[[fn:Philippians 2:6, 10 (KJV).]]

Each rule tests Paul. His promise of the Lord's return within his own lifetime fails the first, since what he foretold never came to pass. Letters that contradict one another, and a life that Acts tells otherwise than Paul, fail the second. A gospel preached as "foolishness" to the wise and a stumbling block to reason fails the third, for it closes the mind that the third rule keeps open.[[fn:1 Corinthians 1:18–23.]]

## Man's innocence

The second principle concerns man. The <em>fiṭrah</em> is innocent: every human is born with a clean slate, and "no bearer of burdens will bear the burden of another".[[fn:Sūrah al-Najm 53:38.]] No sin is inherited, and no guilt passes from father to son. Against this stands Paul's doctrine that sin entered the world by one man and passed upon all.[[fn:Romans 5:12.]] Without a fallen humanity, a crucified redeemer has nothing to redeem.

## The law and the ethic of intention

The third principle concerns the law. ʿĪsā ibn Maryam upheld the law of Moses as God's command, and he restored its purpose. The Qurʾān gives his own words: he came "confirming what was before me of the Torah and to make lawful for you some of what was forbidden to you".[[fn:Sūrah Āl ʿImrān 3:50.]] The worth of a deed lies in the heart that performs it, as the Prophet ﷺ taught: "Actions are but by intentions."[[fn:<em>Ṣaḥīḥ al-Bukhārī</em>, no. 1; <em>Ṣaḥīḥ Muslim</em>, no. 1907, from ʿUmar ibn al-Khaṭṭāb.]] The two belong together: the law kept, and kept from the heart.

Paul broke with both. He declared the law abolished and cursed, and in place of the ethic of intention he set a dogma of salvation by the cross. The ethic of ʿĪsā was turned into its opposite while it kept its name, a transvaluation the charges on this site trace step by step.

## Deeds and the world

The fourth principle concerns action and the world in which it takes place. Man is judged by what he does: "whoever does an atom's weight of good will see it, and whoever does an atom's weight of evil will see it".[[fn:Sūrah al-Zalzalah 99:7–8.]] The world is the theatre of his doing, good in itself and made for his use: "Who has forbidden the adornment of Allah which He has produced for His servants and the good [lawful] things of provision?"[[fn:Sūrah al-Aʿrāf 7:32.]] Its perfection is his task.

Paul taught the reverse on each point. Faith without deeds justifies, the flesh wars against the spirit, and the whole creation groans in travail.[[fn:Romans 3:28; Galatians 5:17; Romans 8:22.]]
