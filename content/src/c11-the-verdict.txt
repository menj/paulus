## What the demographers project

The demographic record already shows the drift. In 2015 the Pew Research Center projected the world's religions from 2010 to 2050. Christianity began the period as the largest religion, with an estimated 2.2 billion adherents, 31 per cent of the world's 6.9 billion people, while Islam stood second with 1.6 billion, or 23 per cent. By 2050 the projection brings the two to near parity, Muslims at 2.8 billion and Christians at 2.9 billion; extended further, the Muslim share equals the Christian share, at roughly 32 per cent each, around 2070, and exceeds it after that.[[fn:Pew Research Center (Conrad Hackett, lead researcher), "The Future of World Religions: Population Growth Projections, 2010–2050," 2 April 2015, overview, https://www.pewresearch.org/religion/2015/04/02/religious-projections-2010-2050/. The projections rest on estimates for 2010; Pew's report of 9 June 2025, "How the Global Religious Landscape Changed from 2010 to 2020," revises the estimates for 2010 and 2020 and offers no new projections beyond 2020, https://www.pewresearch.org/religion/2025/06/09/how-the-global-religious-landscape-changed-from-2010-to-2020/.]]

{{chart:pew-share-2050}}

The losses come by conversion as well as by birth. About 40 million people are projected to switch into Christianity by 2050, and 106 million to leave it, the largest net loss of any religious group.[[fn:Ibid.]]

{{chart:pew-switching}}

In Europe, long the heartland of Christendom, the Christian population is projected to fall from 553 million to 454 million, from three-quarters of the population to less than two-thirds.[[fn:Ibid.]]

The same projections show the religion leaving its old ground. In 2010 a quarter of the world's Christians lived in Europe, and less than 1 per cent in the Middle East and North Africa, where Christianity began; by 2050 Europe's share is projected to fall to about 16 per cent, while sub-Saharan Africa's rises from 24 to 38 per cent.[[fn:Pew Research Center, "The Future of World Religions: Population Growth Projections, 2010–2050: Christians," 2 April 2015, "Regional Change," https://www.pewresearch.org/religion/2015/04/02/christians/.]]
